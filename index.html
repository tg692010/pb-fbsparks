Meet the organizers

Student engineers, launching a district-wide campaign.

Power Breakers, FIRST Tech Challenge Team 22045, has spent multiple seasons designing and building competition robots in borrowed cafeteria space between lunch shifts. This campaign grew out of that experience, and out of conversations with robotics teams at every other FBISD high school who build the same way.

The ask isn't for one team. It's for a shared facility that gives all 11 FBISD high schools the same real access to tools, space, and mentorship that a handful of well-resourced campuses already informally have.

"We've built award-winning robots with almost no dedicated space. We're launching this campaign so the next generation of FBISD engineers doesn't have to."

  • Hands-on engineering experienceSeason after season of building, machining, and competing with almost no dedicated space.
  • District-wide coalitionBuilt alongside robotics teams and CTE staff across all 11 FBISD high schools.
  • Sugar Land rootsA campaign grounded in the community that already invests in championship programs.

Modeled on Katy ISD's Robert R. Shaw Center for STEAM — modular workshop bays, a shared precision-machining lab, and practice space, in the same format as its eight existing robotics bays. No stadium, no bleachers — just year-round space to build. We're also open to repurposing an underused or closing FBISD elementary campus as the site, rather than requiring new construction.

Why it matters

The problem versus the proposed vision

While neighboring Texas districts have invested in dedicated robotics facilities, FBISD teams disassemble equipment daily to make room for lunch.

Current reality

  • Teams lose roughly 45 minutes a day setting up and tearing down makeshift workspaces in cafeterias and hallways.
  • No FBISD campus has access to CNC routers, metal lathes, or waterjet cutters.
  • Robots are calibrated on uneven hallway carpet, causing field-alignment issues at competition.
  • Campuses without well-resourced booster clubs can't afford off-site rental space.

Proposed facility

  • A shared facility with modular team bays, secure tool storage, and fiber internet.
  • A precision-machining lab with CNC mills, laser cutters, and 3D printing, supervised by certified staff.
  • Regulation practice space available year-round to every team.
  • Equal access for all 11 high schools and their feeder middle schools.
Our principles

What guides this campaign

Every ask we make to the Board and the city is built on the same four commitments.

Equity first

Every FBISD student deserves real tool access, regardless of which campus they attend or how well-funded its booster club is.

Real engineering access

Machining equipment and practice space that matches what students will actually use in engineering careers.

Fiscally responsible

Open to repurposing an underused elementary campus instead of requiring new construction from scratch.

Sustained advocacy

A multi-year plan through the FBISD 2027 bond cycle, not a single event.
